Dry Bean Breeding & Computational Biology Program

Department of Plant Agriculture, University of Guelph. Combining decades of breeding expertise with modern computational tools to deliver resilient, high-quality beans for Ontario growers and global markets.

Our History

The Field Bean Breeding Program began in 1977, initially focusing on white bean development with support from OMAFRA, the Ontario White Bean Producer's Marketing Board, and the Ontario Coloured Bean Growers.


First release: Ex Rico 23 (1980) — a navy bean, marking the start of our legacy. A major milestone followed in 2001 with OAC Rex, Canada's first CBB-resistant navy bean from an interspecific cross with a wild species. Both varieties were honored in the 2012 Seed of the Year Award.


In 2009, a consolidated program was formed at Guelph with AAFC. Today, we work alongside AAFC and Guelph researchers to conduct the Ontario Registration and Performance Trials (OPCC), ensuring new varieties thrive in local conditions.

Breeding Pipeline
Each year the program:
STEP 1Makes 200–250 crosses spanning navy, black, cranberry, kidney, pinto, yellow, and adzuki beans
STEP 2Advances populations F2→F5, maintaining broad genetic diversity
STEP 3F5 field selection: up to 5,000 single plants for architecture, maturity, lodging, disease, pod load
STEP 4Lab selection: up to 3,500 plants for seed colour, size, shape, brightness, uniformity
STEP 5Preliminary and advanced yield trials at multiple OPCC locations — assessing >50 traits per line
STEP 62 years OPCC Registration Trials — agronomic merit, stability, market acceptance → registration

🎯 Breeding Objectives for Ontario Growers
1. YIELD IMPROVEMENT
Maximizing productivity to boost farmer profitability. Target: 10–15% yield gain per decade.
2. STRESS RESISTANCE
CBB, Anthracnose (Race 73), White mold, Root rot, drought and heat tolerance. Nematode resistance.
3. NUTRITIONAL QUALITY
Protein, complex carbohydrates, canning quality (hydration coefficient, drain weight, texture). NIRS analysis.
4. MARKET DIVERSIFICATION
11 market classes: navy, black, cranberry, kidney (DRK/LRK/WK), pinto, small red, yellow, kintoki, otebo, adzuki.

📋 OPCC Trial Standards Quick Reference (Version 3.0 · 2025)
📐 Trial Design
• Lattice or RCBD with 4 replications
• Min plot length: 4m after trimming
• Plot labelling: starts at 101, blocks +100
• Seed treatment: Cruiser Maxx Bean + Dynasty
• Randomizations sent by May 15th
• Data due November 15th
• Yield CV >15% (white) / 18% (coloured) = under review
🌱 Seeding Rates
Minor Market Class (85% germ):
• 36cm rows → 13 sd/m = 35 seeds/m²
• 53cm rows → 15 sd/m = 28 seeds/m²
• 76cm rows → 17 sd/m = 22 seeds/m²

Kidney/Cranberry (85% germ):
• 53cm rows → 12 sd/m = 23 seeds/m²
• 76cm rows → 15 sd/m = 20 seeds/m²

• Planting depth: 1–2 inches
• Soil temp: ≥15°C at planting
📅 Assessment Timing (DAP)
• 25–50 DAP: Plot quality notes
• 35–50 DAP: Flowering, flower colour, growth habit
• 35–40 DAP: Root rot rating
• 50–60 DAP: Common bacterial blight
• 45–55 DAP: Bacterial brown spot
• 100–110 DAP: White mold, harvestability, green stem
• 80–100 DAP: Maturity
DAP = Days After Planting (mid-season locations)

⚠️ Herbicide Cautions & Rotation Restrictions
⚠
Imazethapyr (Pursuit) carryover: Do not plant corn for 18 months, cereals for 10 months, canola for 40 months after application. pH-dependent breakdown — alkaline soils extend carryover.
⚠
Fomesafen (Reflex): Do not plant cereals, oilseeds, or root crops within 18 months. Soil pH >7.2 increases carryover risk significantly.
⚠
Sethoxydim + Bentazon tank-mix: Antagonism — reduces grass control. Apply at separate timings (6+ hours apart) or use clethodim instead.
⚠
Clomazone vapour drift: Can bleach adjacent crops. Maintain 200m buffer from sensitive crops in hot weather. CS formulation reduces risk vs. emulsifiable concentrate.
✅
Recommended rotation: Dry beans → corn → soybeans → wheat/barley for maximum herbicide flexibility and disease break. Avoid dry bean on dry bean within 3–4 years to manage white mold sclerotia.

🔬 Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
🍄
White Mold (Sclerotinia sclerotiorum)
Scout at flowering — look for water-soaked lesions on lower stems
Narrow rows (<55cm) and dense canopy increase risk significantly
Fungicides: Cabrio (pyraclostrobin), Allegro (fluazinam) at early flower (R1)
Biological: Contans WG (Coniothyrium minitans) soil application pre-plant
Resistant varieties: OAC Resilient, Mist, Rogue show field tolerance
Rotate out of beans ≥3 years. Avoid bean-on-bean
🦠
Common Bacterial Blight (Xanthomonas axonopodis)
Water-soaked lesions with yellow halos on leaves; pod spots dark-bordered
Spread by splashing rain, infected seed, and equipment
Use certified disease-free seed — seed treatments not fully effective
Copper bactericides (e.g., Kocide) — suppression only, not curative
Resistant UoG varieties: OAC Charm (R), OAC Plasma (R), OAC Rev (S — field notes)
Avoid overhead irrigation during humid weather
🌑
Anthracnose (Colletotrichum lindemuthianum)
Dark, sunken lesions on pods and stems; veins on leaf undersides turn black
Race 73 is predominant in Ontario — check R73 resistance rating
Use certified, treated seed. Thiram seed treatments reduce seedborne inoculum
Fungicides: limited efficacy post-infection; pyraclostrobin preventative
Resistant UoG varieties: OAC Spades, OAC 24-B2, OAC Charm, OAC Bechamel
Rotate ≥2 years. Avoid overhead irrigation
🦟
Bean Leaf Beetle (Cerotoma trifurcata)
Round holes in foliage; 10% defoliation threshold for action
Overwinters in woodland edges — fields adjacent to bush more susceptible
Insecticides: Lambda-cyhalothrin, bifenthrin at emergence if severe
Also vectors Bean pod mottle virus (BPMV)
Scout V1–V6 — early feeding most damaging to stands
🐜
Soybean Aphid (Aphis glycines) on Beans
Clusters on growing tips and undersides of leaves
Economic threshold: 250 aphids/plant on 80% of plants increasing
Natural enemies (Aphelinus, Coccinellids) often provide control — scout before spraying
Insecticides: dimethoate, imidacloprid — avoid spraying during flowering
🫘
Fertility & Soil Health
Rhizobium inoculant (Bradyrhizobium japonicum) — critical if first beans in field >5 years
Target pH: 6.2–6.8 for optimal nutrient availability and nodulation
Phosphorus: 40–60 kg P₂O₅/ha; Potassium: 80–100 kg K₂O/ha
Avoid excess N fertilizer — suppresses nodulation. Max 15 kg N/ha as starter
Zinc deficiency common on high-pH soils — apply 2 kg Zn/ha if pH >7.0
Boron improves pod set — foliar 0.3 kg/ha at early flower if deficient
